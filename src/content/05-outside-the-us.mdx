The royalty sign-ups in this app (ASCAP, BMI, The MLC, SoundExchange) are **U.S. organizations**. If you live and write music somewhere else, you will usually join your own country's versions instead. The ideas carry over; the names change.

## What stays exactly the same

- A song is two things you own separately: the recording and the song underneath it. See [Creation & Rights](/guides/creation-rights).
- Settle splits in writing before you leave the studio. The [split sheet builder](/split-sheet) works anywhere.
- The [release planner](/release), the [red flags](/red-flags) and the [contract check](/contract) work anywhere.
- Distributors deliver your music worldwide, whichever country you are in.

## What changes: the collecting organizations

| This app says | What it is | In your country it is usually called |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PRO (ASCAP or BMI) | Collects performance money for songwriters | A **collecting society** or performing rights organization |
| The MLC | Collects digital copy ("mechanical") money in the U.S. | Often handled by the **same society**, or a separate mechanical society |
| SoundExchange | Collects digital radio money for recordings and performers | A **neighbouring rights** society, often separate for performers and recording owners |

Examples, so you know what to look for: PRS for Music and PPL (United Kingdom), SOCAN (Canada), APRA AMCOS (Australia and New Zealand), GEMA (Germany), SACEM (France). Many countries have one; some have several. Most countries have exactly one that a songwriter is expected to join.

## What to do

1. **Search for your country plus "performing rights society" or "music collecting society."** Use the society's own official site, not an ad or a third-party "registration service."
2. **Check what it collects.** Ask whether it covers performance, digital copies (mechanical) or both, and whether it has a separate arm for recordings and performers.
3. **Check what it costs and who can join.** Fees and rules are set by each society and change, so read the current page. Joining as a songwriter is often low-cost or free, but confirm it.
4. **Join as a songwriter first.** Save the ID number you are given. You will need it on split sheets.
5. **Register your songs** with the society once they are released.
6. **Ask about reciprocal agreements.** Societies pay each other, which is how money from other countries reaches you. A good one will tell you which countries it covers.
7. **Look into neighbouring rights** if you own your recordings, since digital radio money for recordings often goes through a different organization.

## One warning

Anyone who contacts you offering to "register your music worldwide" for an upfront fee is a red flag. Your own country's society, and your distributor, are where this starts. See *pay-to-play and pay-upfront services* in the [red flags](/red-flags).

## Using the rest of this app

The royalty checklist and the steps in the "Royalties & Collection" chapter are written for the U.S. Use them as a map of what needs to be covered, and swap in your country's organizations for the first three.
