A good rollout is mostly **being early** and **being specific**. Almost every missed opportunity comes from starting a week too late.

## Work backward from release day

Pick the date first, then count back. The Release planner does the counting for you and gives every step an exact date.

## Pitching to playlists

Streaming platforms give artists a free way to submit unreleased music to their own editors. It is only available **before** release day, so the submission deadline is the one date you can't make up.

- Claim your artist profile first. See <Portal id="artist_profiles">Spotify for Artists</Portal> and <Portal id="artist_profiles" which="secondaryUrl">Apple Music for Artists</Portal>.
- Write the pitch in plain words: genre, mood, what the song is about, and what you're doing to promote it.
- Pitch one focus track per release.

<Callout title="Don't pay for placement">
Anyone who guarantees playlist spots or stream counts for money is selling something that can get your music removed. See *pay-for-play playlisting* in the [Red flags](/red-flags).
</Callout>

## What to have ready: your EPK

An EPK (electronic press kit) is one page you can send to a booker, blogger or curator:

- a short bio (three or four sentences),
- two or three good photos,
- links to your best songs and videos,
- the release date and a pre-save link,
- contact details.

## Content that costs little

- Cut **teaser clips** (15–30 seconds) from the video you already have. Schedule them across the weeks before release.
- Post the story behind the song, not just "out now."
- Have one clear call to action each time: pre-save, listen or follow. Not three.

## Measure honestly

Look at saves, followers gained and where listeners come from, not just stream totals. Sudden spikes from one place with no new followers are a warning sign, not a win.

## After release day: the first 30 days

Most of the listening a song gets in its first month comes from people you already reach. Use the month on purpose.

**Release day**
- Post one clear message with one link. Not three.
- Message the people who helped (collaborators, engineer, friends) and ask them to share it.
- Submit your recording codes to SoundExchange (see the Royalties chapter).

**Week 1**
- Reply to every comment and message. Early replies are the cheapest marketing there is.
- Post a short clip from the song, plus the story behind it.
- Check your streaming dashboards for where people are finding the song.

**Weeks 2 and 3**
- Share something new about the same song: a lyric, a studio moment, a live or acoustic clip.
- Pitch the song to small blogs or curators in your genre, using your EPK.
- Ask a few fans to save the song and add it to a playlist of their own.

**Week 4**
- Look at what worked: which post got saves, where listeners came from, what people repeated back to you.
- Write it down, then use it to plan the next release. The timeline is already built; start it again.

No tactic here is a promise. Growth is slow for nearly every artist, and the most reliable approach is to put out good music steadily and keep talking to the people who listen.
