A good rollout is mostly **being early** and **being specific**. Almost every missed opportunity comes from starting a week too late.

## Pitching to playlists

Streaming platforms give artists a free way to submit unreleased music to their own editors. It is only available **before** release day, so the submission deadline is the one date you can't make up.

- Claim your artist profile first. See <Portal id="artist_profiles">Spotify for Artists</Portal> and <Portal id="artist_profiles" which="secondaryUrl">Apple Music for Artists</Portal>.
- Write the pitch in plain words: genre, mood, what the song is about, and what you're doing to promote it.
- Pitch one focus track per release.

<Callout title="Don't pay for placement">
Anyone who guarantees playlist spots or stream counts for money is selling something that can get your music removed. See *pay-for-play playlisting* in the [Red Flag Decoder](/red-flags).
</Callout>

## What to have ready: your EPK

An EPK (electronic press kit) is one page you can send to a booker, blogger or curator:

- a short bio (three or four sentences),
- two or three good photos,
- links to your best songs and videos,
- the release date and a pre-save link,
- contact details.

## Content that costs little

- Cut **teaser clips** (15–30 seconds) from the video you already have. Schedule them across the weeks before release.
- Post the story behind the song, not just "out now."
- Have one clear call to action each time: pre-save, listen or follow. Not three.

## Measure honestly

Look at saves, followers gained and where listeners come from, not just stream totals. Sudden spikes from one place with no new followers are a warning sign, not a win.

## After release day

- Submit your recording codes to SoundExchange (see the Royalties chapter).
- Thank everyone in the credits publicly.
- Write down what worked while it is fresh, and reuse the same timeline next time.
