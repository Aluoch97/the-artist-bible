A distributor is the company that delivers your finished music to Spotify, Apple Music and other stores, and collects the money they pay you back.

## What a distributor does (and doesn't)

It **does**: deliver your files, assign codes, collect the recording (master) earnings and pay you.

It **doesn't**, by default: collect your songwriter royalties. Those are collected by other organizations, which is why Chapter 3 matters.

## How to compare distributors

Price is one thing to compare. Ask these questions before you upload anything:

1. **How do I pay?** One-time per release, yearly per release, or a subscription for the whole catalog?
2. **What happens if I stop paying?** Do my releases come down?
3. **How much of the revenue do I keep**, and is there a minimum payout?
4. **Can I leave and take my music with me**, including existing streams and playlists?
5. **Can the earnings be split** automatically between collaborators?
6. **Are any rights transferred** to the distributor? They should not be.

Check each company's current fee page yourself, because pricing changes often. See the *hidden renewal fees* entry in the [Red Flag Decoder](/red-flags).

## The codes that follow your song

- **ISRC** identifies one specific recording. It follows that recording forever, so use the same one wherever that exact recording appears. Your distributor can assign it.
- **UPC** identifies a release (a single, EP or album) as a product.
- **ISWC** identifies the underlying song (the composition). It is assigned after the song is registered with a collection organization.

Mix these up and royalties go to the wrong place, or nowhere.

## Metadata: boring and expensive to get wrong

Metadata is the text that travels with your music: names, titles, credits, dates and flags. Treat it like part of the product.

- **Spell your artist name exactly the same everywhere.** Different spellings can split you into multiple profiles.
- **Primary vs. featured artist:** primary artists are on the release; featured artists are credited on that track. Choose carefully, because it controls whose profile the song appears on.
- **Credits:** list writers, producers and performers correctly. They feed registrations later.
- **Explicit flag:** mark it honestly.
- **Cover art:** square, high resolution, with no URLs, prices or social handles on it. The release checklist uses 3000x3000px.
- **Release date:** pick it early, since editorial pitching depends on it.

<Callout title="Lock the metadata before you upload">
Changing titles, artist names or credits after a release goes live can be slow or impossible on some platforms. Everyone in the credits should approve the spelling first.
</Callout>
