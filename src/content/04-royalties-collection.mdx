"Royalties" is not one pile of money. A single stream on a platform can trigger payments to several organizations, and **each one pays only the people registered with it.** If you aren't registered, your share can sit unclaimed.

## The streams and who collects them

| Money stream | What it pays for | Who collects it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Master (recording) revenue | Streams and sales of your recording | Your distributor |
| Performance royalties (songwriter / publisher) | Songs being streamed, broadcast or performed live | Your PRO (ASCAP or BMI) |
| Digital mechanical royalties | Copies of the song made by streaming services | The MLC (U.S.) |
| Digital radio / neighboring rights | Recordings played on non-interactive digital radio | SoundExchange |

A **PRO** is a performance rights organization. In the U.S., you join **one**: never register the same songs with two.

## Which PRO, and how you get counted

There are three main PROs in the U.S.: **ASCAP**, **BMI** and **SESAC**. ASCAP and BMI are open to songwriters, which is why most independent artists pick one of them. <ResourceLink id="sesac_join">SESAC</ResourceLink> is invitation-only and does not take applications directly, so it is unlikely to be your first stop.

Your PRO can only pay for plays it knows about. It finds out in a few ways:

- **Streaming and digital services** report what was played.
- **TV and film productions** file *cue sheets* listing the music used in a show or movie.
- **Radio plays** are tracked by monitoring services.
- **Live shows** are generally only paid if **you report them** (see below).

Once your songs are registered and a play is reported, the PRO works out what is owed and pays you. Registration comes first, so an unregistered song generally cannot be paid for any of this.

**Music publishing** is the business of managing, protecting and collecting money for songs. Whoever owns the composition owns the publishing, and gets paid when the song is used.

## Playing shows? Report them

Live performances can earn performance royalties, but the PROs usually need you to tell them what you played.

- **BMI:** <ResourceLink id="bmi_live">BMI Live</ResourceLink> lets performing songwriters who are BMI members submit setlists online or in the BMI app. Publishers can't submit them for you.
- **ASCAP:** ASCAP OnStage lets writer members report live shows through Member Access on <Portal id="pro_ascap_bmi" which="secondaryUrl">ascap.com</Portal>. The songs have to be registered with ASCAP first.

There is a time limit on reporting, and it can change, so check your PRO's current rules and report shortly after each show. Keep a simple note of date, venue and songs played so it takes two minutes.

## The order that works

1. **Join a PRO as a songwriter.** You get an IPI number. Keep it.
2. **Register your songs** with that PRO, using the splits you agreed in the studio.
3. **Join The MLC** and connect your songs so digital mechanicals reach you.
4. **Join SoundExchange** as both an artist and (if you own your masters) the rights owner.
5. **Claim your artist profiles** on streaming platforms.

## Common mistakes

- Registering the same song with two PROs.
- Joining as a writer only, then never claiming the publisher side.
- Skipping SoundExchange because "I'm not on the radio." It covers digital radio, not just FM.
- Registering songs without agreed splits, which causes disputes and frozen payments.
