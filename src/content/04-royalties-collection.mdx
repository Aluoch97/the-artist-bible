"Royalties" is not one pile of money. A single stream on a platform can trigger payments to several organizations, and **each one pays only the people registered with it.** If you aren't registered, your share can sit unclaimed.

## The streams and who collects them

| Money stream | What it pays for | Who collects it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Master (recording) revenue | Streams and sales of your recording | Your distributor |
| Performance royalties (songwriter / publisher) | Songs being streamed, broadcast or performed live | Your PRO (ASCAP or BMI) |
| Digital mechanical royalties | Copies of the song made by streaming services | The MLC (U.S.) |
| Digital radio / neighboring rights | Recordings played on non-interactive digital radio | SoundExchange |

A **PRO** is a performance rights organization. In the U.S., you join **one**: never register the same songs with two.

## The order that works

1. **Join a PRO as a songwriter.** You get an IPI number. Keep it.
2. **Register your songs** with that PRO, using the splits you agreed in the studio.
3. **Join The MLC** and connect your songs so digital mechanicals reach you.
4. **Join SoundExchange** as both an artist and (if you own your masters) the rights owner.
5. **Claim your artist profiles** on streaming platforms.

## Common mistakes

- Registering the same song with two PROs.
- Joining as a writer only, then never claiming the publisher side.
- Skipping SoundExchange because "I'm not on the radio." It covers digital radio, not just FM.
- Registering songs without agreed splits, which causes disputes and frozen payments.
